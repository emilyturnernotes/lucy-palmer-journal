My door is hard to move. What should I check first?

Start by looking at the track. Sand, dirt or a small object can prevent the door from gliding smoothly. With the door stationary, remove loose debris with a vacuum or soft brush. Avoid forcing the panel if it catches or feels unstable.

Next, consider how the problem developed. A door that gradually became heavier to move may have worn rollers, a dirty track or an alignment issue. A door that suddenly jams could have debris in its path or damage that needs closer inspection. These are clues, not a diagnosis.

A call about Sliding Door Repair is more productive when you can describe the symptom. Tell the technician whether the door sticks throughout its travel, catches in one spot or feels difficult to lift and slide. Mention scraping sounds and visible track damage, too.

Do I need new rollers or a new door?

Not every difficult door needs a new panel or frame. Rollers support the sliding panel, and worn rollers can make an otherwise sound door difficult to operate. Track condition and alignment also matter, so ask for the cause to be assessed rather than assuming one part is responsible.

For example, a patio door may move reasonably well at first but grind near the middle of the track. Replacing rollers alone may not address a damaged section of track. Conversely, a door with an intact track and worn rollers may be a candidate for Roller Replacement.

What maintenance can I do after a repair?

Simple care can help you notice problems before they become more disruptive. Keep the track clear of loose grit, watch for new scraping and avoid pushing a door that has become unexpectedly difficult to move. Check that the lock engages without excessive force.
